# Documentary Motion Research and Design Direction

## 1. Editorial Research: Techniques Over Channel Skins

This research studies real documentary and explainer editing across six world-class channels/studios: **Vox**, **Dhruv Rathee**, **MagnatesMedia**, **MrBeast**, **Johnny Harris**, and **Lemmino / PBS Frontline**.

The objective is to extract **transferable cinematic editing techniques** rather than superficial branding. For example, MrBeast's high-energy typography should inspire brief, climactic moments of impact in a story, but should never dictate the aesthetic of an entire historical investigation.

### B. Typography Hierarchy and Semantic Emphasis

| # | Channel / Video | Timestamp | Script / Narrative Context | Visual Execution | Transferable Editorial Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | **Vox** — *Explained: Diamonds* | 04:05–04:15 | Narrator discusses De Beers founder Cecil Rhodes. | **Subject-Aware Positioning**: Rhodes is positioned on the right third of the frame. The textual identification slate enters cleanly on the bottom-left third. | Never place graphic overlays on top of human faces or focal subjects. Detect subject bounding boxes and place overlays in opposing safe quadrants. |
| 19 | **MagnatesMedia** | 06:45–06:55 | Showcasing a vintage photograph of a corporate headquarters. | **Framed Containment (No Blur Side-Fill)**: The 4:3 photograph is displayed in a crisp editorial matte frame with subtle drop shadow over dark neutral background. | 20 | **Dhruv Rathee** — *The EV Revolution* | 12:10–12:20 | Footage contains burned-in news broadcast ticker at bottom. | **Safe Area Elevate**: Editorial stats and labels are automatically elevated to the top-left or mid-left zone to avoid colliding with lower-third broadcast tickers. | Detect existing broadcast graphics and subtitles; elevate overlays to uncontested safe zones. |

### E. Entrance, Readable Hold, Exit, and Transitions

| # | Channel / Video | Timestamp | Script / Narrative Context | Visual Execution | Transferable Editorial Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | **Vox** — *Why the US is addicted to highway expansion* | 02:45–02:52 | "Induced demand: more lanes only create more traffic." | **Timed Phasing**: Entrance spring: 0.35s (slide up 20px). Readable Hold: 4.8s (motionless hold). Exit: 0.25s (fade down 15px) before the scene cut. | Graphics must have three distinct phases: fast entrance (0.25–0.45s), static readable hold (>=2.0s), clean exit (0.2–0.3s) before the cut. |
| 23 | **MrBeast** — *Ages 1 to 100 Fight for $500,000* | 01:10–01:14 | "Contestant 45 was just eliminated!" | **Impact Snap & Settle**: Rapid 0.2s scale pop (0.85 -> 1.02 -> 1.0) on impact, followed by a 2.5s calm hold. Audio impact whoosh synchronized with arrival. | Spring physics should settle quickly. Do not keep text constantly wobbling, drifting, or pulsing while the viewer is trying to read. |
| 24 | **Lemmino** — *The Great Silence* | 12:00–12:10 | "If advanced civilizations exist, where is everybody?" | **Type Reveal with Staggered Line Hold**: Headline appears letter-by-letter over 0.6s, then holds dead still for 6 seconds while narrator speaks. | Kinetic text must lock completely once revealed. Never cut away while text is still actively animating. |
| 25 | **Johnny Harris** — *The Border Crisis* | 10:20–10:26 | Narrating a quote from a border patrol agent. | **Quote Reading Duration**: A 22-word quote appears on screen and remains visible for 6.8 seconds (matching ~190 words/min reading speed). | Sizing quote durations strictly by word count: minimum 3.0 seconds, plus 0.3 seconds per additional word over 10 words. |

| 28 | **Johnny Harris** (Visual Grammar) | Full Episode | Balancing technical cartography with human narrative. | **Visual Grammar Continuity**: When comparing two geographic stages of a treaty, the same map projection and color coding are maintained across cuts. | Allow intentional visual continuity: related data points or sequential map stages should retain identical framing for clarity. |

Scene B explains the mechanism. Variety arises from scale, weight, and layout—never from font switching. |

## 3. Current production rules

The user's current editing requirements supersede the earlier reference notes above.
Those notes are aesthetic observations, not measured timing standards.

1. Choose visual ownership from the script: full canvases for explanatory charts,
   diagrams, timelines, and comparisons; supporting footage for brief stats,
   portraits, quotes, and approved copy.
2. Fit text backgrounds to the approved words. Use the director's validated film
   colors and high-contrast ink; protect faces and evidence and avoid wide empty bars.
3. Ordinary highlights last approximately 1.3–2.2 seconds; longer approved copy
   lasts at most 2.4 seconds and chapters at most 2.6 seconds. Quotes and complex
   explanatory graphics get time appropriate to their information.
4. Reset shot-local camera motion at a cut without fading the media from zero.
   Use Remotion's [Sequence](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/sequence) frame-local
   clock for element reveals, followed by a readable hold.
5. Never invent a source, route, value, date, or emphasis phrase. Only verified
   local assets and validated narration-grounded copy/data reach rendering.
6. Compose new scene layouts through the bounded declarative recipe language;
   use reviewed components when they already explain the particular beat.
